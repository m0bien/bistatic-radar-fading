Physical Interpretation of the 2D Multipath Colormap:

Red / Yellow (+6 to +12 dB): Direct and ground-reflected waves arrive in-phase (Δφ = 2πn). Voltage doubles on both links, boosting power by 22 × 22 = 16 = +12 dB.
Green (0 dB): Represents nominal free-space propagation (no multipath enhancement or attenuation).
Deep Blue (≤ −20 dB): Direct and ground-reflected waves arrive 180° out-of-phase (Δφ = π), canceling the signal and creating blind zones.

Reliability & Probability Curves

Why does Outage Probability go to 1.0 (100%) at high SNR?

By definition, Outage Probability is the Cumulative Distribution Function CDF(γth) = P(SNR ≤ γth). It answers: "What fraction of the target trajectory falls at or below required threshold γth?"

  • If you require an impossibly high threshold (e.g. γth = +40 dB), virtually 100% of received signals are below +40 dB, so Outage = 100% (1.0).
  • If your required detection threshold is γth = 0 dB, almost all signals are above 0 dB, so Outage = 0% (0.0).
  • To see the curve that starts at 100% and drops to 0%, select "Coverage CCDF: P(SNR ≥ γ)" above!
